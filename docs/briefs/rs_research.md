# Brief RS: preliminary industry research (one session per pair of industries)

Read `docs/roadmap.md` first. Your session prompt names your industries. Reply to Pablo in Spanish;
dossiers are written in English (internal docs); keep Spanish terms where they are the real names
(e.g. "Máster de Acceso a la Abogacía", "becario").

## Goal

A research dossier per industry that a content writer can turn into excellent pages without
searching again. Audience of the final site: university students in Spain with little idea of
how these industries work. Spain first; add the international route where Spanish students really
use it (e.g. London for investment banking and private equity, US/UK firms' Madrid offices for
Big Law, remote or EU roles for AI and data).

## You own

`docs/research/<industry-slug>.md` for your industries only (slugs in `docs/roadmap.md`). Nothing
else. No code.

## Method

- Use web search and fetch. Prefer primary sources: firm careers pages, official programme pages,
  regulators and professional bodies (e.g. CGAE for lawyers, CNMV), recognised salary and industry
  reports with a year, university career services, reputable press.
- Every factual claim gets a numbered citation `[n]` to a source list at the end (URL, publisher,
  date published or accessed). If two sources disagree, say so.
- Mark each claim's confidence: **verified** (primary source), **reported** (secondary reputable),
  **unverified** (keep only if useful, flagged). Never invent firms, programmes, dates or figures.
- No personal data of private individuals. Public firm and programme names are fine.
- Do not segment advice by year of degree. Frame preparation by starting point and time
  ("12 weeks before applying", "if you have no finance background").

## Dossier structure (all sections required)

1. **Summary** (10 lines): what it is, who it suits, how competitive, typical entry routes.
2. **Segments** of the industry and how they differ (e.g. strategy vs operations vs tech
   consulting; bulge bracket vs elite boutique vs middle market; large-cap vs mid-market vs growth
   PE; ML engineer vs applied scientist vs AI product; analyst vs data scientist vs ML; full-service
   Spanish firms vs Magic Circle vs US firms). Propose which segments deserve their own page.
3. **How the business works:** clients, revenue model, deal or project lifecycle, economics.
4. **Career ladder:** titles by level, typical years per level, exits.
5. **The real job:** a typical week at entry level, tools, skills, hours, travel, culture; myths.
6. **Pay:** entry ranges in Spain (and London/elsewhere if relevant) with source and year only.
7. **Employers in Spain:** a table of at least 20 firms where possible (name, segment, Spanish
   offices, entry programmes, official careers URL). Verify each URL.
8. **Recruiting calendar:** internships, off-cycle, graduate/full-time, by month, Spain and abroad.
9. **Selection process:** stages, tests (online tests, case, technical, modelling, written,
   language), what is assessed at each, typical pass signals, common mistakes.
10. **Preparation plan:** skills to build, a realistic timeline in weeks, how to practise, how to
    show it on a CV; requirements (e.g. law degree + Máster de Acceso + bar exam for Big Law).
11. **Resources:** at least 25 per industry, grouped by type (books, free guides, firm case or
    interview libraries, courses, podcasts, newsletters, communities, university clubs in Spain),
    each with cost (free/paid), language, level and a one-line honest note.
12. **People and networking:** events, associations, how to reach professionals respectfully.
13. **Glossary:** 25+ terms a beginner meets.
14. **Ideas for interactive pieces** that match how this industry hires (3-5 ideas).
15. **Open questions for Pablo** and unverified items.
16. **Sources** (numbered).

## Done when

Both dossiers exist and follow the structure, every factual claim is cited, the employer tables'
URLs were checked, and your report gives per dossier: word count, number of sources, number of
verified vs unverified claims, and the top 5 things a content writer should not miss. Commit in
small steps on your branch; no push, no merge.

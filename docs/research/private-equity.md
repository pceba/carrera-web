## 1. Summary

1. **What it is.** Private equity (PE) funds raise money from investors (pension funds, insurers, family offices), buy companies, improve them and sell them, usually after several years. Juniors source and screen deals, build financial models, run due diligence and support portfolio companies (Warburg Pincus's analyst role: "from sourcing and diligence to portfolio management and exit" [1|V]).
2. **Who it suits.** People who like investing and company analysis, tolerate uncertainty and a slow, relationship-driven hiring process, and are comfortable with fewer entry doors than banking or consulting.
3. **Very few direct doors.** Most European PE firms hire juniors from investment banks (and some from consulting or transaction services) rather than from university; the exceptions run analyst or summer programmes [2|R][3|R][1|V].
4. **Spain is an active but modest market.** SpainCap (ex-ASCRI) reported €6,403M invested in 2025 in 828 deals (+1.8% volume, +5.5% deals); foreign capital was 63.8% of volume [4|R].
5. **Competitive and small.** Teams in Madrid are small; no firm publishes acceptance rates. Michael Page lists Private Equity among the three most requested profiles in Spanish banking/finance [5|V], which signals demand but not volume.
6. **Entry routes:** (a) 1–3 years in IB (or M&A advisory, sometimes consulting/TAS) then a headhunter-driven move [6|V][2|R]; (b) direct analyst programmes at a few global funds (Warburg Pincus Europe: applications open 1 Aug 2026, offers Oct–Nov, start Aug 2027 [1|V]); (c) internships and trainee roles at firms (Permira Madrid listings [7|R]); (d) venture capital and growth as adjacent routes.
7. **Pay (Spain).** Michael Page 2026: PE analyst €35–55K base + 10–40% bonus; associate €55–75K + 20–50%; VP €75–110K; director €110–180K [5|V]. Carry is not included in any source I found.
8. **Hiring is slow and off-cycle in Europe.** London PE windows: September–early December and January–April; interviews ~3–4 months before the start date; most hires have ≥18 months in banking [6|V]. Spain is likely even less structured [6|V says continental Europe "less structured" as an assumption of the interviewee].
9. **Tests.** Final rounds usually include a timed LBO modelling test or case study (1–3 hours) and a paper LBO [8|R].
10. **Proposed pages.** Hub + "Cómo se entra" (routes), "Gestoras en España" (employers by size), "Venture capital" (adjacent), "Test de modelización y LBO" (interactive).

## 3. How the business works

- **Money flow.** A **general partner (GP)**, the management company, raises a **fund** from **limited partners (LPs)**; it invests over ~4–5 years, holds companies several years, then returns cash [standard structure, U; no Spanish source].
- **Fees.** Management fee on committed capital plus **carried interest** (a share of profits above a hurdle) [U standard]. That is why carry can dominate senior pay. The Michael Page tables show salary and bonus only [5|V].
- **Deal lifecycle (junior view):** sourcing and screening; first model and investment memo; due diligence with advisers (the IB/Big 4/law dossiers describe the sell-side); financing (banks/debt funds); signing; **portfolio monitoring**; exit via sale to another buyer, IPO or secondary [1|V for the lifecycle; rest U].
- **Spain 2025 numbers (SpainCap via press):** €6,403M invested (+1.8%), 828 deals (+5.5%); foreign managers €4,082.5M (63.8%, record) in 266 companies; domestic managers €1,888M in 155 deals; ten deals above €100M = €2,176.5M; divestments €3,739M in 390 deals [4|R]. A different estimate (Capital & Corporate) gives ~€7,000M, −1.7% [4|R]: methods differ.
- **Returns.** EY/SpainCap study: aggregate net IRR 11.1% and TVPI 1.6x for Spanish private capital at end-2024 [13|R].
- **Cycle.** Heidrick (Dec 2025): fundraising remains challenging, deployment is slower and holds longer, associate hiring is "steady but measured" [14|V].

## 5. The real job

- **Tasks (verified, Warburg Pincus):** fundraising support; valuations of existing portfolio; exit analysis; thesis development; sourcing and networking; transactions (purchases, exits, add-ons); working closely with portfolio companies, board-observer duties, strategic projects and financings [1|V].
- **Typical week [U synthesis]:** screen CIMs and teasers; update models; prepare an investment-committee paper; calls with advisers; portfolio KPI reviews. Fewer all-nighters than banking in most funds, but deal peaks are intense.
- **Hours:** I found no sourced figure for PE hours; a planned search failed. Do not state any number.
- **Myths:** (1) "PE is easier than banking": no source; (2) "You apply from university": mostly false [2|R][3|R]; (3) "PE = Wall Street films": Spanish PE is mostly mid-market with 10–100M tickets [4|R]; (4) "Carry is guaranteed": carry depends on fund performance (Spanish aggregate net IRR 11.1% [13|R]).

## 9. Selection process

Stages seen in sources (pattern, not a Spanish primary source):
1. Headhunter or direct application; CV and call.
2. First interviews: fit, deals you worked on, why this fund.
3. **Modelling test / case study.** Usually in the final or penultimate round; formats: paper LBO (≈20–40 min), timed 1–3 hour tests, take-home case with a CIM and an investment recommendation [8|R]. Standard tests can include several debt tranches, PIK interest, fees, cash sweep, and a sensitivity (IRR vs exit multiple) [8|R].
4. Final rounds with partners.

**Assessed:** modelling accuracy and speed; assumptions' realism; clear conclusion; reasoning matters as much as the model [8|R].
**Common mistakes (R/U):** time pressure and manual error; weak presentation; not forming a view. Pass signals: clear thesis, simple correct model, explicit risks.
Process length: M&I London says 3 days to 4–6 weeks within a window [6|V].

## 10. Preparation plan

Requirements: strong IB/advisory background for most firms; English and Spanish; modelling. No legal licence to apply [U].

**By starting point:**
- *No finance background:* PE is not the first door. Plan 6+ months for fundamentals (accounting, valuation: see banking dossier), then an IB/advisory or Big 4 deal-advisory role, then 12–18 months before moving [6|V].
- *In an IB/advisory analyst job:* months 0–12 do the work; from ~month 9 prepare: 4 timed LBO tests per month; read recent PE deals; talk to headhunters; prepare 3 deal stories.
- *Wanting a direct route:* target analyst programmes (Warburg Pincus-style) and VC/growth roles; start in July before the August opening [1|V].
- *VC:* build a portfolio of public analyses (startup memos), join student VC clubs, and apply to internships [U].

**Practice:** timed LBO tests (Financial Edge 90-minute test, M&I 60-minute test, Wall Street Prep 1-hour test and 4-hour advanced tests [8|R]); write an investment memo for a Spanish listed or private company; read GP reports.
**On a CV:** deals worked on, models built, memo or stock pitch, clubs.
